# Hooks and retention

Retention is the leak, not the click. Average view duration on the first six videos was 30 to 131 seconds. Most viewers leave in the intro.

## The first 60 seconds

| Seconds | Do |
|---|---|
| **0 to 3** | Pattern interrupt: a result, a moment or a sharp claim. Never a welcome. |
| **3 to 10** | Confirm the click. The viewer should know the title is being delivered. |
| **10 to 25** | Name the common belief, flip it, open a loop that pays off later. |
| **25 to 60** | Linh's one-line credibility, the two camps, the number of lessons. |
| **After the first payoff (~minute 3)** | The only place for a subscribe line, in one sentence. Optional. |

**Order of teaching: 2-1-3-4.** Teach the second-best point first, the best second. It earns the viewer's trust before the big idea.

## Hook types (pick one per video)

| Type | Pattern | Example from Linh's material |
|---|---|---|
| **Moment** | A specific scene from Linh's day, then the result | "Today, while I was driving, I asked my AI assistant, 'Which dentist did I go to last week?'" |
| **Cost** | Name what the viewer is already paying | "The cost of doing everything yourself isn't money." |
| **Confession** | Linh names the pattern in herself first | "I was the one saying 'it's easier if I do it myself.'" |
| **Contrarian** | Flip a belief the viewer holds | "Hiring more people will not fix your business." |
| **Number** | A specific result | "It saves her about 17 hours a month." |
| **Villain** | Name an enemy | "Task Delegator trap", "accidental success" |

**Preferred for this channel:** Moment, Cost, Confession. They use disclosure before diagnosis.

## Audience scenes that make strong hooks

- A client call during a family holiday
- Texting the team under the table at a school event
- Dinner with the phone face up
- "Once things settle down..." said for the third year in a row
- Health, marriage and friendships on hold

## Hook gates

A hook passes if:

- [ ] The first line contains a person, a place, a number or a result.
- [ ] No "welcome", "hey guys", "today I'll be talking about".
- [ ] It confirms the title within 10 seconds.
- [ ] It opens a loop the viewer wants closed.
- [ ] Linh could say it in one breath.

## Keeping people past the intro

- **Name the loop and pay it off.** "I'll tell you the part she got wrong in lesson 2."
- **Chapters as reasons to keep watching.** Not labels like "Lesson 3". Use "Why it took her a full day to build".
- **A pattern interrupt every 60 to 90 seconds.** A quote, a cut to b-roll, an on-screen line.
- **Do not repeat the promise.** Say it once in the hook.
- **Tighten.** 8 to 12 minutes. Four of the first six videos ran 13 to 18.5 and bled viewers.
- **Open the next video's loop** in the close, and name it.

## What to avoid

- The channel pitch before minute 3
- A credential list before the first payoff
- A title promise the script delivers after minute 5
- "In this video, I'm going to..."

## Test a hook

Read the first 25 seconds aloud and ask: would a tired founder with a phone in one hand and a coffee in the other stop for this? If not, rewrite. The `hooks-and-titles` skill generates variations and gates them.

Related skills (outside this repo, if installed): `hook-writer` for 20+ variations.
