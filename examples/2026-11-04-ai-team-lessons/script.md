## LESSON 1: Start with one boring, repeated job (1:00–2:45)

One of my team runs our finance reporting. Every week she used to pull the data out of Xero, export it and build the reports by hand. Then she'd check and re-check her own work, because with numbers you have to be accurate.

Now, after she matches payments in Xero as usual, Claude drafts about 80% of the reports for her. She's built separate reports for each team, so the numbers make sense to the people reading them. She worked out it saves her about 17 hours a month on report preparation.

But here's the part I want you to hear. She said the biggest benefit wasn't just the time. It was her stress and anxiety. Before, she was going from one thing to the next and couldn't even think. Now she has headspace to ask better questions.

And before she set any of it up, she did an audit of everything she does. Her goal was clear: save time, and get time to think.

Her advice to you was simple:

"Pick one report you build every week manually, hand it over to Claude, teach it every week how to improve, and it will do the rest."

I do this myself too. Our team fills out a weekly huddle entry, and AI now populates mine automatically. I used to go through my emails manually trying to remember what I did all week!

That's what buying back time looks like. It doesn't come from one big transformation. It comes from one repeated job at a time.

**Your exercise:** Write down three tasks you or your team do every single week the same way: a report, a recap, a follow-up. Circle the one that drains you the most. That's your first AI project.

## LESSON 2: Treat AI like a direct report, not a magic button (2:45–4:45)

`[ON SCREEN TEXT: AI is only as good as the data you give it]`

This was the most honest part of the whole huddle.

My finance lead said: AI is only as good as the data you give it. It will give you a report that sounds confident, but you still have to check it.

Her mindset is to treat AI like a direct report. You check its work, you get comfortable asking it questions, and the better your questions, the better you train it. Her rule is to verify three times and be discerning.

And it does get things wrong. Another part of my team shared the mistakes they caught:

- feedback attached to the wrong person
- a recording matched to the wrong client
- a tone that came across rude or blunt, or sounded obviously AI-written

So what did they do? They didn't give up. They wrote wording rules into the instructions, which they call "skills", so the AI gets it right next time. They also caught a limit in one of their tools, and they changed the setup so it checks email as well as Notion.

Even with my own huddle entry, I still go in and edit it, because sometimes it's too thorough!

**Your exercise:** For the task you circled, write a short "how I like it done" note, just like you would for a new team member. Include your tone, your format and the three things it must never get wrong. That becomes your AI's training.

## LESSON 3: Connect your tools so the work flows (4:45–7:00)

Once you're comfortable with one task, the next level is connecting the dots between your tools.

Our client success team used to finish every call by summarising it, copying, pasting and emailing it out. Now when a call is recorded in Fathom, a workflow kicks off. It drafts the follow-up email, logs it in Notion and leaves it in the inbox for a person to review.

One of my team described what it does now. It finds the right client card in Notion, suggests a date for the next call, writes notes for the next conversation and drafts a coaching summary. She said, "All I do is, yes, book it."

`[B-ROLL: workflow / Notion board]`

Another part of my team used to open Notion, Gmail, Fathom and Drive one by one just to prepare for a check-in call. Now Claude builds one prep pack for them.

They also connected Claude to Google Chat. Instead of opening every chat and retyping the same message, they write it once. It goes to each person in their own chat, personalised with their name.

But let's be real about the setup. One of my team said it took her more than a day to build, because the workflow runs through eight or ten different steps. And it's never finished. When someone noticed we weren't asking happy clients for Google reviews enough, she added a step to flag it.

Her words: "It's not set in stone. You're always working around and playing with it."

**Your exercise:** Pick one thing that happens after every meeting or sale in your business. Write down every step and every app it touches. Then mark which steps actually need your judgement. The rest can be connected.

## LESSON 4: Use AI to build capability, not just speed (7:00–8:45)

Speed is great. But my favourite story was about capability.

Our marketing team used to be reactive. We'd meet each month and ask, "What do we post?" Now they've used ChatGPT to build out our ideal customer profiles, their pain points and our core messaging. Every quarter, it helps create the content plan, and it always comes back to who we're actually talking to.

Then my marketing lead did something brave. She took our paid ads in-house. She created custom GPTs for each part of the job, including a "paid media specialist" that guides her through setting up campaigns, testing creative and, most importantly, understanding why something is or isn't working. That part used to feel daunting to her. Now she can do it.

I do a version of this myself. My AI assistant can put on a different hat: a financial planner, a legal assistant. I upload what's relevant and it starts helping me in that role.

And the result? It isn't just faster captions. It's a more capable team with more time for strategy and thinking.

**Your exercise:** Where are you currently stuck waiting on outside expertise, or avoiding something because it feels too hard? Write it down. Then ask yourself what it would look like to create an AI "specialist" that walks you through it step by step.

## LESSON 5: Make it a team habit, not a founder hobby (8:45–9:45)

Here's what I learned as the founder. If AI only lives in your head, your business won't change.

So we're making AI experience sharing a regular part of our monthly huddle: half the time for connection, half for sharing what we've learned.

I also asked my team who rates themselves eight out of ten or above with AI, so everyone knows who to go to. Then I encouraged everyone to pick a buddy and learn from each other, instead of waiting for an expert to show them.

And one of my team admitted, after seeing everyone else's setups, "My morning brief is so plain!" That's exactly the point. When people share, everyone levels up.

**Your exercise:** Book 30 minutes in your next team meeting for an AI share. Ask each person one question: "What's one thing you used to do by hand that AI now helps with, and what did it get wrong?"

## RECAP (9:45–10:15)

1. Start with one boring, repeated job.
2. Treat AI like a direct report: check it, question it, train it.
3. Connect your tools so the work flows, and keep a human on the final yes.
4. Build capability, not just speed.
5. Make it a team habit.

AI won't give you your life back on its own. But used intentionally, it gives you and your team something precious: time and headspace to think about what matters.
